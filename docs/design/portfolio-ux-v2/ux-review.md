# Kaushik Kuberanathan — portfolio UX review

**Live site:** https://kaushikkuberanathan.github.io  
**Repo:** kaushikkuberanathan/kaushikkuberanathan.github.io (`main`, last pushed November 9, 2026)  
**Also glanced at:** GitHub profile README (kaushikkuberanathan/kaushikkuberanathan)  
**Review date:** November 13, 2026, America/New_York  
**Scope:** Review only. No pushes, branches, PRs, deploys, or spend.

Screens were captured from the live site at desktop 1440×900 and mobile 375×812. Recommended frames use the site’s colors, type, and published copy. No new metrics were invented. Unpublished details (trade-offs, team size, talks) are marked as placeholders. Email addresses are not shown on the boards.

---

## Verdict (five lines)

The site already has the raw material of a strong product-leadership portfolio: real outcomes, named stories, colleague recommendations, shipped side projects, and a clear “open to Lead / Group / Director” signal. What it does not do is put that material in the order a visitor actually decides. In the first ten seconds a recruiter can read a name, a four-part title, and five skill chips — not 5M+ MAU, +32% YoY eCommerce growth, or 4× order growth — and LinkedIn plus the résumé are either missing or gated. A hiring manager who stays for two to five minutes will find depth, but will have to dig through tabbed essays and long paragraphs to learn how he frames a problem, what he traded off, or how big the team was. A peer looking for a point of view on AI-assisted product work will eventually find a good one — “Treat AI collaboration as a real operating discipline, not a shortcut” — parked as principle 6 at the bottom of Overview, with the essays reduced to a title list on Contact. The craft of the site is careful; the first-run experience undersells the leader it is trying to introduce.

## Top friction points (ranked by visitor impact)

1. **The first screen does not close the 10-second test.** *(Recruiter, also Hiring manager)*  
   A visitor sees a long title, five skill chips, and muted “open to” copy. The published proof — 5M+ MAU, +32% YoY, 4× orders, $35M — lives later, and the Metrics tab explicitly tells people to look elsewhere for those headline numbers. On mobile, eight tabs wrap into four rows, “Open to…” is clipped, and every action is below the fold. The GitHub profile README is actually tighter than the live hero: it leads with 5M+ MAU and an “At a Glance” table.

2. **Contact is incomplete on the first screen, and the résumé is gated.** *(Recruiter, Hiring manager)*  
   LinkedIn — the default recruiter and hiring-manager move — is only on the Contact tab, and Contact has no résumé button. “Get in touch” is email, but the visible label does not say so (and the accessible name is “Email Kaushik,” which does not match the visible text). Clicking “View résumé” on a first visit opens a well-written email gate (“Enter your email to view the résumé — I’ll also send you a copy”) instead of the PDF.

3. **Stories are outcome paragraphs, not cases a hiring manager can interrogate.** *(Hiring manager)*  
   The Cox commerce story has the ingredients of a flagship case (5 steps to 2, +32% YoY, 4× orders, 5M+ MAU) packed into one sentence, next to redacted conversion rates. No story states a trade-off, a team size, or a split of ownership. Experience is long (about 4,000 pixels on desktop) and rich, but it still reads as a list of accomplishments rather than a view of judgment.

4. **The point of view is buried, and the writing is a title list.** *(Industry leader / peer)*  
   The best published AI stance is operating principle 6, below the fold on Overview. Essays sit on Contact, after “Get in touch,” as titles only — so a peer cannot tell a product thesis from a personal essay without leaving the site. No talks are listed.

5. **The information architecture works against every persona.** *(All three)*  
   Eight primary tabs, including a password-gated “Confidential Projects” tab in the third slot. The hero — the only `h1`, the name, and the primary actions — is hidden on every tab except Overview (`hero.hidden = true`). A deep link into Experience or Impact starts with no identity and no way to contact him. Inserting the Confidential tab at runtime also helps wrap the mobile nav and contributes to a 0.272 cumulative layout shift on mobile.

## Recommendations

Each item is tagged **Quick win**, **Medium**, or **Big**, and with the persona(s) it serves. Copy suggestions use only published language, or a clearly marked placeholder.

### 1. Rebuild the first screen around title, proof, and three actions — **Medium** — Recruiter, Hiring manager

Keep the visual system. Change the content order.

Suggested hero copy:

> **Kaushik Kuberanathan**  
> Lead Product Manager, Digital Commerce & Platforms  
> Open to senior product leadership (Lead / Group / Director) · Atlanta, GA  
> I turn complex, regulated customer problems into scalable product systems — 15+ years across digital commerce, self-service, and enterprise platforms.

Put four already-published tiles under that line: **5M+ MAU**, **+32% YoY** eCommerce growth, **4×** order growth, **$35M** cloud modernization. Drop or demote the five skill chips. Make the actions **Email for roles**, **LinkedIn**, and **View résumé**, and keep them visible on every tab with a slim persistent identity bar.

See board `01-hero.png`. The nav finding is also in this change: five tabs (Overview · Impact · Experience · Lab · Contact), not eight.

### 2. Put the headline numbers on the Metrics / Impact view — **Quick win** — Recruiter, Hiring manager

Stop telling visitors the big figures are “already covered elsewhere.” They are not on Overview. Lead the proof surface with 5M+ MAU, +32% YoY, 4× orders, ~96% containment, ~75% faster title turnaround, and the $35M program — all already on the site — then supporting stats, then outcome-led stories.

See board `02-proof.png`.

### 3. Rewrite flagship stories as problem, decision, trade-off, role, outcome — **Medium** — Hiring manager

Start with the Cox native-commerce story. Keep the published outcome as the headline. Add a problem and a decision from copy already on the site (link-out dependency, 5 steps, invest in native in-app purchase with consent and catalog governance). For the two gaps a hiring manager will ask about, use placeholders until he publishes them — do not invent:

- `[Trade-off not yet published — what was deprioritized or accepted, and why]`
- `[Team size and what engineering, design, legal, or other PMs owned — not yet published]`

Keep exact plan-management conversion rates reserved for a conversation, as the live site already does. Repeat this pattern on Equifax and Cox Automotive.

See board `03-casestudy.png`.

### 4. Take the email wall off the résumé, and put LinkedIn next to Email — **Quick win** — Recruiter, Hiring manager

Open `Kaushik Kuberanathan - Resume.pdf` in a new tab with no form. If lead capture is still useful, make it a follow-on: “Want a copy in your inbox? Email me a copy.” Add LinkedIn to the hero and add “View résumé” to Contact. Change the visible label “Get in touch” to “Email for roles,” and make the accessible name match the visible text (this also fixes a Lighthouse / axe `label-content-name-mismatch` on that button and on “See what I’ve shipped”).

See board `04-contact.png`.

### 5. Make the mobile first screen do the job — **Medium** — Recruiter

One row of five tabs, not four rows of eight. Name, level, four proof tiles, and Email / LinkedIn / Résumé all have to fit on a 375×812 first screen. Make the actions at least 44px tall (today they are 35px; they pass WCAG 2.2 AA’s 24px target, but they fall short of a comfortable 44px touch size). Moving Confidential Projects out of the primary nav should also remove most of the 0.272 mobile layout shift, which is caused by the hero being pushed down when that tab is inserted.

See board `05-mobile.png`.

### 6. Feature the point of view, and give each essay a thesis — **Medium** — Industry leader / peer

Promote principle 6 into a “Point of view” section a peer will actually see:

> Treat AI collaboration as a real operating discipline, not a shortcut.  
> Structured formats for requirements, decisions, and executive communication — with an explicit devil’s-advocate check on every recommendation and a clear line between confirmed fact and inference.

Then list the three essays with one-line theses taken from the pieces themselves — and keep *The Number 65* labeled as a personal essay, not a platform thesis. Sit the 60%+ enterprise AI-workflow practice next to the writing. If he has talks or podcasts, add them; until then, use a placeholder: `[Talk or podcast appearance]`. Do not invent appearances.

See board `06-pov.png`.

### 7. Smaller fixes worth doing in the same pass — **Quick win** — All three

- Keep a persistent name + Email / LinkedIn / Résumé bar when the visitor leaves Overview, so the only `h1` and the contact path do not disappear.
- Move Confidential Projects to a “Request a deeper brief” path on Contact. It is a reasonable gate; it is not a third primary tab.
- Point the Dugout “Repo” link at a public URL, or drop it. `https://github.com/kaushikkuberanathan/lineup_generator` is a private repository and 404s for visitors.
- Update the footer date (it still says September 2026).
- Load Inter (it is in the font stack but never requested, so the live site falls back to system UI).
- Replace the 16×16 solid-navy favicon with a real mark, add a `rel="icon"` link, and add a 1200×630 Open Graph image (`twitter:card` is currently `summary` with a 389×500 portrait headshot).
- Add `:focus-visible` styles for tabs and links (today only the résumé-gate input and a couple of tooltips have a visible focus ring), and a skip link to the main content.
- Align the GitHub profile README “At a Glance” table with the live hero, so the two public surfaces tell the same 10-second story.

## Technical findings

**Performance.** The site is genuinely fast. Playwright measured first contentful paint at 0.42s and a ~28KB HTML transfer. Lighthouse desktop (November 13, 2026): Performance 100, Accessibility 100, Best Practices 100, SEO 100. FCP / LCP 0.4s, TBT 0ms, CLS 0.003, total byte weight 449KB. Lighthouse mobile: Performance **86**, other categories 100. FCP / LCP 1.1s. The mobile ding is almost entirely **CLS 0.272**, with the hero (`header#top`) as the culprit — consistent with the Confidential Projects tab being inserted into the wrapping nav at runtime.

**Accessibility.** Images have alt text; `lang="en"` is set; headings are in order on Overview; contrast work has already been done (footer and gray pills were raised to meet 4.5:1). Remaining issues:

- No skip link. Lighthouse marks `bypass` as not applicable; keyboard users still tab through eight tabs before any content.
- `label-content-name-mismatch` on “Get in touch” (`aria-label="Email Kaushik"`) and “See what I’ve shipped” (`aria-label="View builder projects"`).
- Almost no `:focus-visible` on tabs or links.
- On every non-Overview tab the hero is hidden, so the page has no visible `h1` (the repo’s own axe gate already notes `page-has-heading-one` as a moderate on Product Lab and Contact).
- Touch targets are 34–39px. That passes WCAG 2.2 AA (24px). It does not meet a 44px comfort guideline.
- `--muted-2` (#8a94a6) is 3.06:1 on white and fails AA where it is used as text.
- Inter is declared but never loaded.

**Links.** During the live pass:

- `https://github.com/kaushikkuberanathan/lineup_generator` returns **404 to the public** (the repository exists and is private). This is a real broken visitor link.
- `coaching-photo.jpg` and `dugout-screenshot.png` 404, but both are optional / commented or unused in the live markup, so they do not currently show as broken images.
- LinkedIn URLs returned 999 and the Automotive News citation returned 403 from this environment; treat those as bot-blocking, not confirmed broken for a signed-in person.
- `https://locado.app` redirects unauthenticated visitors to login, which is expected for the app, not a dead link.
- Dugout Lineup, Substack, GitHub profile, and the custom GPT links resolved.

**Metadata and polish.**

- Title, description, and `og:url` / `og:image` are present. There is no canonical tag, no `theme-color`, no `apple-touch-icon`, and no explicit favicon `<link>` (browsers still fetch `/favicon.ico`).
- The favicon is a 16×16 solid `#172033` square — a dark tab, not a mark.
- `twitter:card` is `summary`, and the share image is a 389×500 portrait, which will letterbox badly in large-card slots.
- No analytics are present. That may be intentional; it also means there is no evidence of how recruiters actually move through the tabs.
- No console errors or page errors on load.
- The résumé PDF is a 5-page letter document (September 10, 2026). Length is worth a separate pass; it was not audited in this review.

**Profile README consistency.** The profile README is, in some ways, a better 10-second pitch than the live hero: it leads with 5M+ MAU at Charter / Spectrum, an “At a Glance” metrics table, Dugout Lineup, and badges for the portfolio, LinkedIn, and email. It does not mention Locado (the live Product Lab does), and it does not link the résumé. The two public surfaces should tell the same story.

## What is already working

- The writing is specific. Outcomes have denominators, time windows, and sources, and a few figures are honestly withheld instead of rounded into mush.
- Colleague recommendations are named, titled, and linked to the public LinkedIn recommendations view.
- Product Lab shows real shipped work (Dugout Lineup live, Locado live, a paused project honestly marked), not a wallpaper of logos.
- The visual system is calm and consistent, and the desktop layout does not overflow. Load time is excellent.
- Deep links and in-page evidence buttons already work. The information is there; it is the first-run order that fails the visitor.
